BirdCLEF+ 2026: 234 species, classical audio features

A Kaggle competition: say which of 234 Pantanal species are calling in a five-second passive recording. The training audio is clean close-ups, the test is a noisy mixture. A hand-built feature vector and one CatBoost per species reach 0.737 on the leaderboard, and the report spends most of its effort on why that is where they stop.

Three datasets that don't match

Before any model, the report maps what exists and where the test will differ from the training data.

What is provided

All audio is OGG Vorbis, mono, 32 kHz. The difficulty is not the format but how each set was recorded.

Base 1
train_audio
35,549 recordings from xeno-canto (23,043) and iNaturalist (12,506). One primary label each, variable length (0.03 s to 549 s, median 19.6 s). Covers 206 of the 234 classes.
Base 2
train_soundscapes
1,478 five-second segments from passive Pantanal recorders, with a multi-label vector. Covers 75 classes, 4.2 species per segment on average and up to 10.
Test
About 7,200 five-second segments from the same passive recorders. One probability per class, scored by the mean AUC-ROC over classes, so a rare insect counts as much as a common bird.

Three shifts between training and test

Focal vs passive
Base 1 clips aim a microphone at one animal. The test is autonomous recorders capturing everything in range. A model that learns a clean signal is later asked to find it inside a mixture, so a validation split drawn from Base 1 would flatter it.
Two sources
xeno-canto gives a quality rating from 1 to 5 (mostly 4 or 5). iNaturalist gives none, all its files read 0, so quality cannot be filtered the same way on both. About 12 % of files, xeno-canto only, carry secondary labels.
World vs Pantanal
97 % of Base 1 was recorded outside the Pantanal. About 70 species may sing differently there, so the model can learn dialects it will not hear at test time.

Three acoustic signatures

Birds
Short modulated syllables separated by silences. Needs dynamics, which averaging flattens.
Insects
Continuous wide-band signal with no temporal structure.
Amphibians
Continuous too, but in a narrow band. Global statistics are enough for insects and amphibians, so the features have to mix static and dynamic information.

Most energy sits below 8 kHz, so audio can be resampled from 32 kHz to 16 kHz without losing what matters.

A hand-built feature vector

Version 1 bets on analysis over model size: no neural network, only what the data called for.

The pipeline

  1. ResampleTo 16 kHz, justified by the spectral analysis.
  2. CutNon-overlapping 5 s segments, capped at 20 per file so a ten-minute file does not over-represent its species. Base 1 gives 198,437 segments, Base 2 keeps its 1,478.
  3. Describe170 numbers per segment, mean and standard deviation of each: MFCC with delta and delta-delta, spectral centroid, bandwidth, rolloff, flatness and contrast, RMS and zero-crossing rate, chroma.
  4. One-vs-RestOne binary classifier per species, each outputting a probability. Positives are re-weighted automatically, with scale_pos_weight capped at 100.
  5. CatBoostDepth 6, learning rate 0.05, L2 regularisation 3 and early stopping: a robust compromise rather than a tuned optimum.

What version 1 showed

Learnable classes work
On the 47 shared classes CatBoost averages 0.742 and some species pass 0.98.
A ceiling by construction
The 28 Base-2-only classes stay at 0.5 on all three models because Base 1 holds no positives for them. They drag the macro AUC down mechanically.
Anti-predicted classes
Three classes score below 0.4 (trsowl, 116570, magant1) despite hundreds to thousands of training examples. That is the typical symptom of a strong focal to soundscape gap.
Uneven by group
Mammalia 0.84, Aves 0.75, Amphibia 0.68, Insecta 0.50, Reptilia 0.43: plain statistics do not capture the same information in every kind of signal.

A dead end: BirdNET embeddings

BirdNET v2.4 produces a 1024-dimensional vector per 3 s and was trained largely on xeno-canto, so it looked like a natural fit. On CPU it took about two seconds per focal file, hours for Base 1 (the report later estimates over twenty), and batching and parallelism did not help. The idea was dropped to keep a pipeline that already scored well and had room to improve.

Version 2: more signal, more data

Three changes stacked on top of version 1, each answering something the first run exposed.

Three cumulative changes

307 features
Skewness and kurtosis of the MFCCs for temporal dynamics, eight mel bands (mean and standard deviation) to separate stationary insect and amphibian signatures, and a silence rate to tell dense focal files from airy soundscapes.
Multi-label targets
Secondary labels, present in 12.2 % of Base 1 files, turn each target into a 234-dimensional binary vector, which adds positives for species that co-occur.
Train on both bases
Base 2 matches the test distribution but is tiny and easy to overfit, so it is split by soundscape file: 52 files (1,182 segments) join training, 14 files (296 segments) stay out for an honest hold-out. This unlocks the 28 Base-2-only classes, and the report counts 229 trained classes.

Why only +0.009 on Kaggle? Half of the newly unlocked classes stay stuck near 0.5, the insects are hard to tell apart with averaged statistics, and the 47 shared classes were already the best predicted, so their gain weighs little in a macro AUC pulled down by the hard ones. The public score also covers only part of the hidden test, so the private gap may be wider.

Where the features run out

Three classes that version 2 does not fix, each for a different reason.

Two-colored oval frogdomain gap
Amphibia, 0.85 → 0.52
The focal clip is dominated by continuous broadband noise around 2 kHz with a few low-frequency pings. The soundscape shows regular horizontal bands between 2 and 5 kHz, probably a chorus of several individuals. In version 1 the model only met soundscapes at evaluation time and found chance correlations. Once trained on soundscapes, it learned that its focal signature was far from the annotated ones.
Southern Spectacled Caimantoo little data
Reptilia, 0.43 → 0.55
Its calls are short, powerful low-frequency bars, but it rarely calls: most positive soundscape segments are ambient noise with a faint signal. Base 1 holds a single focal file, so learning a decision boundary from one example is statistically impossible. The 26 positives in Base 2 lift it slightly and then it plateaus.
Rococo Toadmasked by others
Amphibia, 0.87 → 0.59
A steady call around 500 to 1,000 Hz is its focal signature. In soundscapes that drone is buried under bird and amphibian transients, and statistics averaged over five seconds cannot isolate it. Version 2 exposes a problem version 1 was hiding: the features cannot separate overlapping sources.

The report's reading: the pipeline copes when a species' signature stays consistent between focal and soundscape (most birds and mammals) and unlocks Base-2-only classes mechanically. It fails on rare classes, on signatures drowned by other sources, and where averaged statistics miss what is specific to a species. These are structural gaps, not tuning problems.

What a version 3 would try

Ranked by expected return on effort, from the report's perspectives. None of this was run.

  1. Pretrained embeddingsBirdNET (1024-d), Perch (1280-d) or AVES in place of the 307 statistics. They help most on the focal to soundscape transfer and on masked signals, but need GPU time or a one-off chunked extraction. Little help for the caiman, whose problem is data.
  2. Targeted augmentationMix soundscape background into focal files and stack several focal clips to imitate co-occurrence. Nearly free and aimed straight at the domain gap, so it should ease the amphibian regressions.
  3. Non-audio metadataA geographic prior from the species' training locations against the Pantanal, a seasonal filter (the test is February to March), and a cheap test of quality against quantity: train on xeno-canto rating 3 or more plus iNaturalist.
  4. Ensembling and tuningA geometric mean of XGBoost and CatBoost probabilities, then a short Optuna search on the hard classes. Sure but marginal, kept for last.
